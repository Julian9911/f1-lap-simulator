# Decision log

Short records of the engineering decisions that shaped the model: what was decided, why, and what it costs. Most recent first within each group. Version numbers refer to `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Modelling scope

### D-01 Rigid-body 3-DOF dual-track vehicle (from v1)
**Context.** The goal is a credible lap-time simulator, not a full vehicle-dynamics study.
**Decision.** Use the Vehicle Body 3DOF Dual Track block: planar motion (longitudinal, lateral, yaw) with individual wheel loads, no suspension, no ride height.
**Consequence.** Fast, robust and easy to explain. No pitch, roll or ground-effect behaviour; aero is lumped (Cl, Cd, Af, no balance).

### D-02 Lateral tire force from a saturating slip-angle table (from v1)
**Decision.** Mapped slip-angle mode with Cy(alpha) tables (front peak 1.7, rear 1.8) generated by `f1_lateral_tire_table.m`; longitudinal force from a Magic Formula wheel.
**Consequence.** Lateral force saturates and the front axle gives way first (stable understeer). The block scales lateral force linearly with load (Fz / Fznom): no load sensitivity of grip.

### D-03 No ERS (v6)
**Context.** The 2026 car deploys at most 350 kW from the MGU-K and about 4 MJ per lap (about 11 s of full deployment in an 88 s lap); the ICE gives about 400 kW.
**Decision.** Do not model the ERS.
**Why.** The model already delivers about 700 kW continuously, which is more average power than the real car. A realistic, energy-limited ERS would most probably slow the lap down, so it would add complexity and worsen the headline number without improving fidelity. `f1_ers.m` is kept as a starting point, unconnected.
**Consequence.** Declared limitation; the engine map stands for the electrified power train as a whole.

### D-04 Wet track, tire compound and wear not integrated (v6)
**Context.** A wet-track switch was designed: a single `gripScale` factor (0.82, calibrated to Pirelli's crossover from slicks to intermediates at 110-112 % of the dry lap time).
**Decision.** Not integrated.
**Why.** It needs about six edits in the model (two control blocks and the four wheels), and compound and wear need grip to become a signal. The cost and risk did not justify it for this version.
**Consequence.** Dry track only. The prototype is not part of the repository; only the harmless `gripScale` factor remains in the setup scripts (value 1.0 reproduces the dry result).

## Dynamics and control

### D-05 Friction circle in the torque and brake limiters (v3)
**Context.** With more grip in the speed profile, a rear wheel spun on corner exit and another locked under braking. The limiters assumed full grip was available, but in a corner the tire already uses most of it laterally.
**Decision.** Limit traction and braking to `mu_x = min(mu, sqrt(mu_pk^2 - mu_y^2))`, with `mu_y` estimated from the lateral load transfer.
**Consequence.** Lap time 109.29 s to 102.96 s and no wheel spin or lock-ups. Setting `useFC = false` reproduces v2 exactly (checked).

### D-06 Aerodynamics: ClA 2.7 to 4.8 m2 (v4)
**Decision.** Raise Cl from 1.8 to 3.2 (ClA 4.8 m2), inside the 3.2-6.4 m2 range of public sources, together with `FZMAX` 8600 to 15 000 N (the wheel loads reached about 10 kN).
**Consequence.** Gap to the real lap from +14.9 s to +6.6 s. Wheel lock-ups disappeared (both changes were made together, so the individual contributions are not separated).

### D-07 Engine at about 750 kW with an 8-speed gearbox (v5)
**Context.** The engine map delivered about 1 MW; scaling it to 750 kW with one gear made the lap 10 s slower, because a single ratio kept the engine far from its power peak for most of the lap.
**Decision.** Add the gearbox (gears 2-8, ratios from telemetry, shifts at 11 000 / 8 500 rpm) first, then scale the map by 0.91.
**Consequence.** 95.28 s with realistic power and transmission, against 94.71 s with 1 MW and one gear. The speed profile now computes its acceleration table from the same engine, gearbox, aero and rolling data instead of measuring it.

### D-08 Limited slip differential at 2500 N preload (v4)
**Decision.** Replace the nearly open differential with an LSD.
**Consequence.** No measurable effect on lap time from 500 to 12 500 N preload (95.18 / 95.16 / 95.24 s). Kept because it is closer to the real car; the preload is an unsourced assumption, declared as such.

### D-09 Active aero straight mode through the external force port (v6)
**Context.** The 2026 car has a corner mode and a straight mode; with one aero configuration the top speed was 302 km/h against 317 km/h real. Cd and Cl of the Vehicle Body block are fixed during a simulation.
**Decision.** Keep the block as the corner mode and inject the drag difference through the `FExt` port, `dFx = 0.5 rho v^2 (CdA_corner - CdA_straight)`, with CdA_straight = 0.85 m2 and a hysteresis on lateral acceleration. The speed profile uses the same 0.85 m2.
**Why 0.85.** It is a calibration (range 0.8-0.9) to the real 90th-percentile acceleration at 70-90 m/s, not a published number. Matching the remaining difference with drag alone would need CdA of about 0.6, which is implausible.
**Consequence.** Top speed 316 km/h. Only drag is modelled; the downforce reduction on straights is not. Lap time moved from 95.28 s to 94.91 s; applying the change to the car only gave 94.56 s, and keeping the profile coherent with the car cost 0.35 s (cause not investigated).

## Track and validation

### D-10 Calculated race line, not the real GPS path (v6)
**Context.** Local speed differences with the real lap (about 500 m, 4050 m, 5500 m) trace back to differences between the OSM-derived line and the real trajectory (for example corner radius 79 m against about 32 m at 5500 m).
**Decision.** Keep the calculated minimum-curvature line.
**Why.** The project is built only from public data and computes its own geometry; using the real path would turn part of the validation into an input. It also uses information a real team has and this project does not.
**Consequence.** Known, documented geometry mismatches. A diagnostic option exists (`silverstone_raceline_real.csv`, switch `raceFile` in `f1_load_raceline.m`); it was never run in the simulator.

### D-11 Compare against the real lap by position, not by integrated distance (v2)
**Context.** A first comparison by integrated distance was offset by 85-165 m.
**Decision.** Align the real telemetry to the circuit by position (ICP alignment, mean distance 2.3 m) and compare on a common distance grid.
**Consequence.** Reliable per-sector and per-corner comparisons. Sector boundaries (1660 m, 4420 m) are approximate, taken from an image.

### D-12 Regression test with a baseline and tolerances (v2)
**Decision.** After any change, run an automated PASS/FAIL script with a lap-time baseline and thresholds.
**Consequence.** A broken model is detected in seconds. The baseline must be updated deliberately when the model is improved on purpose.

## Housekeeping

### D-13 Retired prototypes (v6 clean-up)
Removed `f1_axle_tire_simple.m`, `f1_wheel_tire.m` and `f1_wheel_tire_slip.m` (single-axle and simplified wheel models from before the dual-track design) and `f1_compare_real.m` (replaced by `f1_telemetry.m`). Confirmed by running the regression without them.
